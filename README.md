## 1. Project Overview

W3aver is an independent website discovery platform that provides an alternative way to explore the internet beyond mainstream social media, centralized content platforms, and engagement-driven recommendation feeds.

The project addresses a growing problem in the modern web: independent websites are increasingly difficult to discover.

Although the internet contains an enormous number of independently created websites, personal blogs, technical resources, scientific publications, open-source projects, creative experiments, and small online communities, most users discover only a relatively small portion of this content.

Online discovery is heavily influenced by major platforms, search engines, social networks, and algorithmically personalized recommendation systems.

W3aver aims to provide an additional discovery layer for the independent web.

Its purpose is to connect people with websites that already exist elsewhere on the internet, making independent online projects easier to discover without requiring their creators to depend exclusively on mainstream distribution platforms.

## 2. The Problem W3aver Addresses

The central problem addressed by W3aver is the lack of accessible discovery channels for independent websites.

Several related issues contribute to this problem:

### 2.1. Concentration of online discovery

A relatively small number of large platforms act as primary gateways to online information and content.

Independent website owners frequently depend on these platforms to attract visitors, build an audience, and distribute their work.

This makes it harder for smaller websites to reach people who might genuinely find them useful or interesting.

### 2.2. Algorithmic content discovery

Many popular platforms prioritize recommendation systems designed around engagement, retention, and behavioral metrics.

Although these systems can help users find relevant information, they also influence what receives visibility and can create repetitive discovery experiences.

W3aver explores a different approach: focusing on the discovery of independent websites rather than reproducing an engagement-maximizing social media feed.

### 2.3. Low discoverability of independent websites

Independent developers, researchers, writers, enthusiasts, and small publishers frequently maintain websites that have limited visibility.

Examples include:

- Personal websites.
- Independent blogs.
- Developer portfolios.
- Open-source projects.
- Scientific and educational resources.
- Technical documentation.
- Experimental web applications.
- Independent digital publications.
- Creative coding projects.
- Niche online communities.
- Digital gardens and personal knowledge bases.

These websites may offer substantial value despite having relatively small audiences.

W3aver aims to help people discover these resources.

### 2.4. Dependence on external distribution platforms

Creating an independent website is only part of the process of publishing online.

Attracting visitors can require maintaining profiles across several social networks, following platform-specific trends, producing frequent updates, and competing for algorithmic visibility.

W3aver provides another possible distribution channel, centered on the original website rather than a creator's social media presence.

## 3. How W3aver Works

W3aver is designed around link-based website discovery.

People can share independent websites by submitting their URLs, titles, and supporting contextual information, such as descriptions or visual previews.

Submissions help introduce websites to other visitors who are exploring the platform.

The original website remains the destination.

When visitors discover something interesting, they follow the link and access the external website directly.

W3aver therefore operates as a discovery layer rather than a conventional content-hosting platform.

The distinction is fundamental:

- W3aver helps users discover websites.
- Independent websites remain hosted at their original locations.
- External websites retain their own identity and presentation.
- W3aver provides links and contextual information rather than replacing the original destinations.

Visitors can explore shared websites without being required to register or create an account.

The platform is intended to remain lightweight and focused on discovery.

## 4. Product Principles

W3aver is guided by several principles that distinguish its intended experience from conventional social media.

### No advertisements

W3aver follows an advertising-free philosophy.

The discovery experience is not intended to depend on displaying advertisements or monetizing visitor attention.

### No artificial paid reach

Independent website visibility should not depend on purchasing promoted placement.

W3aver aims to provide an alternative discovery channel for websites regardless of their owners' advertising budgets.

### No engagement-driven recommendation model

The project's philosophy does not prioritize maximizing screen time, compulsive scrolling, or engagement-based content distribution.

The central purpose is to help people discover interesting websites.

### Independent destinations

W3aver does not aim to replace the websites it introduces.

Independent websites remain independently maintained and accessible at their original URLs.

### Accessible browsing

Browsing should not require creating an account or providing personal information simply to explore the available websites.

### Lightweight discovery

The project focuses on URLs, titles, descriptions, and visual previews rather than hosting entire external content experiences.

W3aver is conceptually related to the Indie Web and Small Web.

These concepts emphasize the importance of independent online spaces, personal websites, individual publishing, and a diverse web that is not entirely dependent on centralized platforms.
